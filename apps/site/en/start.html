Get started

Open Kiwi

  • Click the Kiwi icon on the browser toolbar to open the extension's own search page.
  • Type k and a space in the address bar, then your search.
  • Open s.kiwi.moe.

Make it the default search

A browser does not let an extension change its default search engine, so you add it once yourself:

  1. In Chrome open chrome://settings/searchEngines and click “Add” next to “Site search”.
  2. Name it Kiwi Search, give it the shortcut kiwi and the address https://s.kiwi.moe/#q=%s.
  3. In the entry's ⋮ menu choose “Make default”.

In Firefox use the “Add” button next to “Default Search Engine” in the Search settings, with the same name, address and keyword. Safari cannot take a custom address as its default search; open s.kiwi.moe, or make it your homepage.

Choose the search interface

The interface can come from the website s.kiwi.moe or from the page inside the extension; choose in the settings under “Get started”. With the website, your search is written after the # in the address and the browser does not send it to the website. With the extension's own page, the browser does not contact the website at all and the website cannot use the extension. If the website does not open, click the Kiwi icon on the toolbar.

Start the browser on Kiwi

The extension does not take over the new-tab page. To start on Kiwi, put https://s.kiwi.moe/ into your browser's own settings; menu names depend on your browser version.

  • Chrome, Edge: under “On startup” choose “Open a specific page”, or turn on the home button under “Appearance” and enter the address. Without an extension there is no way to make the new-tab page itself open an address.
  • Firefox: under “Home” choose “Custom URLs” for the homepage and new windows. The new-tab page can only be Firefox Home or a blank page.
  • Safari: set the homepage to this address under “General”, then choose “New tabs open with: Homepage”.
